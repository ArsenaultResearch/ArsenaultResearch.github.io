Winged and wingless queens have coexisted in *Leptothorax* for a long time, across diverse ecosystems and through dramatic shifts in life history. How is such a polymorphism stably maintained?

The most obvious explanation is dispersal: winged queens can colonize distant sites, while wingless queens stay close to home, and a mix of the two may help colonies persist in harsh, patchy habitats. It remains unclear, however, whether this simple dispersal hypothesis can explain the polymorphism across all the contexts where it occurs.

To test this hypothesis, we developed artificial nests that *Leptothorax AF-erg* and related species prefer over natural nest sites, and that other ant genera do not colonize. These nests let us sample live colonies passively and non-destructively across the species' range in Canada and the northern United States, with far more replication and spatial resolution than was previously possible.
