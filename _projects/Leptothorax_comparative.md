Shifts in life history, such as moving into a new habitat, becoming a social parasite, or changing reproductive mode, are often accompanied by sweeping changes in the genome. Sex chromosomes are the best-studied example, but whether the same patterns hold for genetic polymorphisms more broadly is unknown.

The *Leptothorax* genus group is an ideal system to answer this question. Our preliminary work suggests that the *ergatoid* supergene, which produces wingless queens in *Leptothorax AF-erg*, is both genetic and ancestral across the clade. Over a history of only a few million years, these ants have moved into specialized habitats at least twice, evolved social parasitism at least four separate times, and repeatedly changed their mating systems and colony organization. Remarkably, the queen polymorphism persists, with modification, through all of these transitions.
